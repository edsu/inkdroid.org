[Archival Poetics and the War on Memory] (Naropa University, 2012) is a panel discussion about the conditions that produce, sustain and delimit archives, and how these processes are intrinsically connected to (or born out of) poetics and creative practice. The panel included a talk from [Stacy Szymaszek] where she shared her *Archival Poetics Manifesto*, which I wasn't able to find on the web. Since it's 14 years old at this point, I thought I'd make it more searchable with a pointer to the recording.

---

## Archival Poetics Manifesto by Stacy Szymaszek

I, the poet, as subversive strategy, leave behind evidence of my day-to-day production.

I, the poet, contend with fear, aphasia, and tendency to forget in order to claim my unclaimed experience, which is one definition of trauma.

Poem as archive, where I claim my experience, where I store my data.

I, the poet, in order to tell my story, take on the identity of others, refuse a temporally fixed past.

I, the poet, as Hart Crane, Ann Pasolini, Izza panther, am an ancient Egyptian boy, is a mariner.

I, the poet, am compelled to tell most of my story as a man. Stories that can or must repress other stories stories that can or must stretch beyond history.

I, the poet make history by living at the scene of the crime where homosexual bodies were and are brutalized.

I, who dress myself up in carrion inhale their lexicon.

I, who cannot be killed in a post-victim state.

I, poetry in the form of a rose.

I, the queer in the gaps of their biographies, where they must sense that I materializes to find expression and demand reparation.

I, the poet, disorganizes time in order that we can and must experience extreme bodily pleasure.

It is a stretch beyond history.

Divine mimesis, divine mimesis, divine mimesis, the right magmatic key to the interstices.

Culture produces a surfeit of memory.

It is hard to remember most of my life under these conditions.

But that we share a timing, a weapon in hell.

But that I, the poet, incorporate you, a prison of dead bodies on live ones.

But that we share a timing.

But that we share a timing.

The nation is a space of struggle, I mean the empire.

I, the poet hold affects that threaten self-cohesion. I cannot or must not dissociate. So the empire dissociates me from national psyche. Split from a reality where the empire is the only author.

I, the poet confess this is a painful way to live.

I, the poet administrator am responsible for an archive. Just in case it could last. Someone who is recording everything, a recording activist, we record everything.

I, the poet administrator, is maker of event, assert public, assert memory, a public made so by recording.
Business model assumes the infrastructure must be weak, so I, the poet administrator of sound mind, has become an activist of infrastructure, maker of poet body that is not weak.

Thousands of hours of audio of poets voices each a specter a haunted archive where the underrepresented withstand the onslaught of history.

I, the infrastructure activist gets equally emotional over financial records haunted by an abacus left brain, right brain, well greased, gaps in organizational memory decisions made with incomplete understanding not leaving traces for poet administrators that will provide transformative relation with their present.

I, the poet administrator sleep under an archive that is physically safe though so many others do not.

We, the poet makes more archive the kind that goes underground with its people and the kind for the library.

At war within organizational memory, the war within oral history, we the poet archive interprets utterance layers of meaning build to now where I, the poet administrator, talk about it as history am implicated in what and whose knowledge and power, whose dailiness is left out.

I absent myself from the archive, keep thin files until I become someone else, then I inhabit the archive, eroticize the archive, feel haunted by bliss.

I, the poet administrator, confess this is a painful way to live.
