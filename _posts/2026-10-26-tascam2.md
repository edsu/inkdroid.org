*TL;DR Yes, user forums and the Internet Archive are extremely important for digital preservation work. LLMs also seem quite good for synthesizing information that is found in them, connecting the dots between aspects of the problem you are facing, and connecting you to other people engaged in similar work.*

---

[Back in June] I wrote about how my brother John had died, and how among the things he left behind were over 100 CD backups of music he had created in the early 2000s. Each CD was clearly labeled with a song title, and contained a multi-track backup of a single song, which (I was to learn) was made with a TASCAM 788. He had told me he had these backups years ago, but I'd never seen them until we were emptying out some of the storage space in his apartment building.

John preformed live in different small venues in Pittsburgh in the 1990s, and then in the Doylestown, PA area in the early 2000s. He left behind several guitars, and other musical equipment. But despite my encouragement on many occasions he never really released any of his music in any form, except for one song on Bandcamp, that he had recorded much later in 2022. John's music was really important to me, even more now that he is gone, so I wanted to see if I could retrieve these songs, and perhaps put together a collection for family members, and other people who were interested in hearing his music, and remembering his creative spirit.

Unfortunately the CDs use a proprietary format, that can only be read using the TASCAM 788's optional CD burner. As I noted in the previous post I did figure out a way to rip the raw data from the CDs and reverse engineer a program to read the tracks, as well as the final mix. It is far from perfect because the gaps between tracks are not consistent. Sometimes the program would extract part of a song as a track. It also wasn't clear what the final mix was. Still, it is possible to pull the concatenated tracks off as a single WAV file, which was amazing to hear. But I was hopeful I could get the tracks as they existed in the backups.

John left his TASCAM 788 in the storage unit too, along with an (optional) external CD burner for creating the backups. My brother Matthew actually thankfully kept it. However the devices looked quite different in style, and I think I mistakenly thought the CD recorder was part of some old computer equipment, and donated to a local record store, along with part of his CD collection. Once I realized the CD recorder's significance for recovering John's music it was too late to get it back. So I bought one on Ebay for a little less that \$200.

Once I got the CD burner hooked up to the TASCAM 788 I discovered that I could read the CDs and see the tracks, and listen to them, but not export them. While it's possible to export each track by hand by connecting the audio output directly to a soundcard with digital input, there are over 100 CDs, so I was hoping I could easily extract the track and the final mix as a WAV file. My goal is to be able to view these in another [DAW] on my laptop instead of learning how to use the 788 itself.

In conversation with Claude I learned that I could [upgrade the firmware] from v1.10 to v2.02
to get a new Export to WAV function on the 788, and that would let me easily burn the tracks to a CD, which I could then read on my laptop. Claude also pointed me at a [TASCAM forum] where the upgrade was being discussed. Unfortunately the *other* TASCAM site where the upgrade was located was now gone, and while [the page] was in Wayback Machine, the actual ZIP file it linked to was not. While I could've pieced some of this information together given enough time it was actually very useful to learn about it in the context of my questions and conversation.

One of the helpful people on the forum seemed to be responding over the years to requests for the firmware update file. So I responded on the forum asking if anyone still had it available. I ended up hearing back about a month later, and was able to flash the 788, and like magic, it now has a new Export to WAV function. Since [lots of copies keeps stuff safe] I placed a copy of the [788 V2.02 Firmware Update] up at the Internet Archive.

But, alas when I went to use the Export function it assembled the WAV file on the TASCAM but then gave a PANIC message when actually trying to write to the CD. I tried a few times hoping it was a temporary glitch, but it failed with the same result at the same time. Again according to Claude apparently CD-RW devices can retain the ability to read, but no longer be able to write due to a weakening [laser diode]. The CD-RW I found on Ebay did seem kind beat up honestly. So I rolled the dice and dropped another \$175 on Ebay in the hopes that this one would work better. I'm still waiting for it to arrive.

I guess if this new one doesn't work as well I will fall back to using the reverse-engineered Python script to extract WAV from the raw CD rip and work with that instead. It has been therapeutic spending time with these backups, especially because it was clear that John created them knowing they would be accessed some day. It also is heartbreaking, thinking that he's no longer around to talk to about them.
